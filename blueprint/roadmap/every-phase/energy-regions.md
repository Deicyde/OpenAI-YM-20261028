# Zero-energy and low-energy regions

Write a point of $\mathbb R^4$ as $p = (p_0, p_1, p_2, p_3)$. The last
coordinate $p_3$ is the time coordinate; when $\mathbb R^4$ is read as
energy-momentum space, the space on which the energy-momentum measures of
Wightman data live, $p_3$ is the energy.

The **zero-energy region** is the hyperplane

$$
Z = \{ p \in \mathbb R^4 : p_3 = 0 \}.
$$

For every real number $u$, the **low-energy region** of height $u$ is the slab

$$
\Lambda_u = \{ p \in \mathbb R^4 : 0 < p_3 \le u \}.
$$

The definition allows every real $u$; for $u \le 0$ the region $\Lambda_u$ is
empty. The spatial coordinates $p_0, p_1, p_2$ are unrestricted in both sets.
