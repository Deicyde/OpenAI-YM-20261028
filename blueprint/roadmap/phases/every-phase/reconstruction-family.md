# Family of reconstructions over the phases

Fix a Wilson family, an extraction and a limit table $S$ (the unselected
Euclidean correlation table), with common label space $\mathbb F$, and a
record $\mathcal F$ for them. For every degree $d$ the graded fields of degree
$d$ form the quotient label space $F_d$ of $S$; the label of $\beta \in F_d$ is
its image in $\mathbb F$, and $\mathbb F$ is the span of these images. Inner
products are conjugate-linear in the first argument. Of the record the
definition uses:

- the hierarchy of $S$ (its Euclidean axioms), including its reflection and
  reality symmetry, which defines on every $F_d$ the adjoint under time
  reflection;
- the field algebra $\mathcal A$: inclusion and derivative maps $\partial$
  between the graded fields, rotations $R$ and a Euclidean stress tensor $T$;
- the phase selection: an Osterwalder–Schrader space of $S$ with slab
  operators; a compact metric space $X$ with its Borel σ-algebra, whose
  points $s$ are called characters, a measure $\nu$ on $X$, the phase
  measure, and for every $s \in X$ a character (a star-homomorphism to
  $\mathbb C$) of the closed star algebra generated by the compressions of
  these slab operators to the ground space; for every
  $s \in X$ a phase table $S_s$; a selected character $s_0 \in X$; and an
  Osterwalder–Schrader space $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_*$ of $S_{s_0}$ for the
  time axis with slab operators $\Pi_*$ on it;
- the continuation $(\mathsf F_*, W_*)$ of the selected phase table
  $S_{s_0}$, with its boundary distributions;
- the selected Wightman data $\mathfrak W_*$ for $S$, with star operation
  $a \mapsto a^\star$ on $\mathbb F$ and Lorentz action $\sigma$ (the maps
  $\sigma(g)$ of $\mathbb F$, for Lorentz transformations $g$, together with
  the maps of each $F_d$).

A point of spacetime is $q = (q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3) \in \mathbb R^4$, with time
$t(q) = q_3$ and spatial part $\vec q = (q_0, q_1, q_2)$; a momentum $p$ has
energy $p_3$. For a configuration $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and heights $y$,
$t(x) + \mathrm i y$ is the tuple of complex times $t(x_j) + \mathrm i y_j$
and $\vec x$ the tuple of spatial parts. The data below use these notions,
each explained in mathematical words.

- An **Osterwalder–Schrader space** of a Euclidean correlation table for the
  time axis is a Hilbert space with a vector for every word of positive
  times, whose span is dense and whose inner products are the time-reflected
  Schwinger functions of the table, together with a self-adjoint contraction
  semigroup of time translations $(e^{-\tau H^{\rm OS}})_{\tau \ge 0}$ (a
  name for the semigroup; no generator enters) and a commuting, strongly
  continuous unitary group of spatial translations that act on these vectors
  by translating the words. Its Euclidean vacuum $\omega$ is the vector of
  the empty word.
- **Slab operators** on it assign to every thickness $\ell \ge 0$ and word $w$ a
  bounded operator that maps the vector of a positive-time word $b$ to the
  vector of $w$ followed by $b$ translated by $\ell$ in time, whenever $w$ lies
  in the time slab of thickness $\ell$.
- **One-point slab operators realized by** slab operators $\Pi$ assign to
  every half-width $\delta \ge 0$ a complex-linear map $a \mapsto B_\delta(a)$
  from $\mathbb F$ to bounded operators, such that for $\delta > 0$, every
  degree $d$ and every $\beta \in F_d$, $B_\delta$ of the label of $\beta$ is
  $\Pi(2\delta, w)$ for the one-point word $w$ at the spatial origin and time
  $\delta$ carrying the fixed representative of $\beta$ (the representatives
  form a fixed linear section of the quotient $F_d$). The **Euclidean word
  vector** of such operators at half-width $\delta$, labels
  $a_1, \dots, a_n$ and complex times $t(x_j) + \mathrm i y_j$ with spatial
  positions $\vec x_j$ is obtained from $\omega$ by applying $B_\delta(a_n)$,
  then the complex-time evolution and spatial translation from the
  $(n-1)$-th to the $n$-th point, shortened in Euclidean time by $2\delta$,
  then $B_\delta(a_{n-1})$, and so on, and finally, after $B_\delta(a_1)$,
  the complex-time evolution and spatial translation from the origin to the
  first point, shortened in Euclidean time by $\delta$.
- A **continuation** consists of functions
  $\mathsf F(z, \xi; a_1, \dots, a_n) \in \mathbb C$ of $n$ complex times
  $z \in \mathbb C^n$, $n$ spatial positions $\xi \in (\mathbb R^3)^n$ and $n$
  labels in $\mathbb F$, for every $n$, and of boundary distributions
  $W(a_1, \dots, a_n; \cdot)$ on Schwartz functions of $(\mathbb R^4)^n$,
  both multilinear in the labels.
- **Wightman data** for $S$ consist of a Hilbert space $H$, a field domain
  $D \subseteq H$, a vacuum $\Omega \in D$, smeared fields
  $\varphi(a, f) : D \to D$ for $a \in \mathbb F$ and Schwartz test functions
  $f$ on $\mathbb R^4$, depending complex-linearly on $a$ and on $f$, a star
  operation (a conjugate-linear map $a \mapsto a^\star$ of $\mathbb F$), a
  Lorentz action (for every Lorentz transformation $g$ an invertible linear
  map of $\mathbb F$, the action on labels, and an invertible linear map of
  each $F_d$, the action on graded fields), a strongly continuous unitary
  representation $V$ of the Poincaré group on $H$ and an energy–momentum
  projection-valued measure $E$ on $\mathbb R^4$.

**Definition.** A **family of reconstructions over the phases** for
$\mathcal F$ assigns to every character $s \in X$ the following data and
properties.

1. An Osterwalder–Schrader space $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$ of the phase table
   $S_s$ for the time axis.
2. Slab operators $\Pi_s$ on $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$.
3. The reconstruction input at $s$: $S$ and $S_s$ satisfy the Euclidean
   axioms; $S_s$ respects $S$, that is, in every degree the null space of
   $S$ is contained in that of $S_s$; the inclusion laws of $\partial$ hold
   for $S$ and for $S_s$ and its derivative laws hold for $S_s$; and
   $\Pi_s$ satisfies the slab laws relative to $\partial$ (copy bounds,
   smoothness in the positions of the points, and the rule that
   differentiating in a point's position replaces its label by the
   derivative label).
4. A continuation $(\mathsf F_s, W_s)$.
5. Compatibility of $\Pi_s$, $\mathcal A$, $\sigma$ and the time-reflection
   adjoint of $S$ with this continuation:
   - (a) it is a continuation of $S_s$: $\mathsf F_s(\cdot\,; a_1, \dots, a_n)$
     is continuous where the imaginary parts of the times strictly increase
     and holomorphic in the times there, its values at purely imaginary,
     strictly ordered times are the Schwinger functions of $S_s$ read on
     labels of $S$, and for every strictly increasing height tuple $y$ and
     Schwartz function $h$ the function
     $x \mapsto \mathsf F_s(t(x) + \mathrm i y, \vec x; a_1, \dots, a_n)\, h(x)$ is
     integrable and its integral with $y$ replaced by $\varepsilon y$ tends
     to $W_s(a_1, \dots, a_n; h)$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$;
   - (b) these tested values tend to $W_s(a_1, \dots, a_n; h)$ as $y \to 0$ through all
     strictly increasing height tuples (non-tangential boundary values);
   - (c) there is a reconstruction staging over $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$ and
     $\Pi_s$ (one-point slab operators realized by $\Pi_s$ with polynomial
     bounds, and a continuation with matrix-element, holomorphy, Euclidean
     value, growth and regularity properties) whose continuation functions
     are $\mathsf F_s$, and for every star operation on $\mathbb F$ that is
     the time-reflection adjoint of $S$, the staging satisfies the analytic
     compatibility conditions with $\sigma$, that star operation and $W_s$
     (invariance of $W_s$ under translations and Lorentz transformations,
     the Fourier–Laplace link between $W_s$ and the staging's continuation,
     the support of the Fourier transform of $W_s$, a holomorphic extension
     to the forward tube, exchange data at small heights for adjacent pairs,
     faithfulness of $W_s$ on labels, convergence of the Gram matrix of
     smeared Euclidean word vectors to $W_s$, and covariance of these vectors
     under translations), and the reflection Gram identity holds: inner
     products of two Euclidean word vectors of the staging equal its
     continuation at the reflected-and-joined configuration, with starred
     labels on the reflected side;
   - (d) some Wightman data for $S$ satisfy every Wightman condition except
     uniqueness of the vacuum, relative to $W_s$, $R$ and the reflection and
     reality symmetry of $S$;
   - (e) every Wightman data with that property have Lorentz action
     $\sigma$ (on labels and on graded fields) and admit exactly one
     bounded complex-linear map into $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$ with the
     boundary-isometry property.
6. Wightman data $\mathfrak W_s = (H_s, D_s, \Omega_s, \varphi_s, V_s, E_s)$
   for $S$ whose star operation is that of $\mathfrak W_*$ and whose Lorentz
   action, on labels and on graded fields of every degree, is $\sigma$.
7. A proof that $\mathfrak W_s$ satisfies every Wightman condition except
   uniqueness of the vacuum, relative to $W_s$, $R$ and the reflection and
   reality symmetry of $S$:
   - the star operation is the time-reflection adjoint of $S$ on every degree;
   - the Lorentz action on labels is a representation and comes from the
     linear actions on the graded fields of each degree; these graded-field
     actions are continuous in the Lorentz transformation, have as boost
     derivatives $-\mathrm i$ times the rotation generators of $R$ in the
     time-space planes, agree with $R$ on spatial rotations and commute with
     the time-reflection adjoint;
   - $\|\Omega_s\| = 1$, $D_s$ is dense and spanned by the vectors
     $\varphi_s(a_1, f_1) \cdots \varphi_s(a_n, f_n)\, \Omega_s$, and
     $f \mapsto \varphi_s(a, f)\, \psi$ is continuous for $\psi \in D_s$;
   - $\langle \varphi_s(a, f) \psi, \chi \rangle = \langle \psi, \varphi_s(a^\star, \bar f) \chi \rangle$
     for $\psi, \chi \in D_s$, with $\bar f$ the complex conjugate test;
   - $\langle \Omega_s, \varphi_s(a_1, f_1) \cdots \varphi_s(a_n, f_n)\, \Omega_s \rangle = W_s(a_1, \dots, a_n; f_1 \otimes \dots \otimes f_n)$;
   - fields whose test functions have spacelike separated supports commute
     on $D_s$;
   - every nonzero label $a$ has a test function $f$ with
     $\varphi_s(a, f) \ne 0$;
   - for every Poincaré transformation $g$, $V_s(g)$ maps $D_s$ into itself and fixes $\Omega_s$, and
     $V_s(g)\, \varphi_s(a, f)\, \psi = \varphi_s(\sigma(g)\, a, f \circ g^{-1})\, V_s(g)\, \psi$,
     where $\sigma(g)$ is the action on labels of the Lorentz part of $g$;
   - for every $\psi \in H_s$ and $c \in \mathbb R^4$,
     $\int e^{\mathrm i\, c \cdot p}\, d\langle \psi, E_s(p)\, \psi \rangle = \langle \psi, V_s(c)\, \psi \rangle$,
     where $c \cdot p$ is the Euclidean inner product and $V_s(c)$ the
     translation by $c$;
   - $E_s$ vanishes on the complement of the closed forward cone.
8. A bounded complex-linear map $J_s : H_s \to \mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$.
9. The boundary-isometry property of $J_s$: $J_s$ is isometric,
   $J_s \Omega_s = \omega_s$ and $J_s$ intertwines the translations of
   $\mathfrak W_s$ with those of $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$ (real-time
   evolution combined with spatial translations); some one-point slab
   operators are realized by slab operators on $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$; and
   for every $n$ and labels $a \in \mathbb F^n$ there is a continuous linear
   map $\Phi_a$ from Schwartz functions on $(\mathbb R^4)^n$ to $H_s$ with
   $\Phi_a(f_1 \otimes \dots \otimes f_n) = \varphi_s(a_1, f_1) \cdots \varphi_s(a_n, f_n)\, \Omega_s$,
   such that for all slab operators on $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_s$, all
   one-point slab operators they realize and every Schwartz function $h$, the
   Euclidean word vectors of half-width $\delta$, labels $a$ and complex
   times $t(x_j) + \mathrm i y_j$, smeared as $\int h(x)\, (\cdot)\, dx$ over
   $x \in (\mathbb R^4)^n$, converge to $J_s \Phi_a(h)$ as $y \to 0$ with
   $0 < y_1 < \dots < y_n$, $0 < \delta < 1$, $4\delta < y_1$ and
   $4\delta < y_{j+1} - y_j$.

At the selected character the family takes the selected data:

$$
\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_{s_0} = \mathcal H^{\rm OS}_*, \qquad
\Pi_{s_0} = \Pi_*, \qquad
(\mathsf F_{s_0}, W_{s_0}) = (\mathsf F_*, W_*), \qquad
\mathfrak W_{s_0} = \mathfrak W_* ,
$$

the second equality read through the first, as an equality of slab
operators on the same space. No character is excluded: items 1 to 9 hold at
every $s \in X$. Uniqueness of the vacuum is not part of the definition, and
$J_s$ is not required to be onto.
